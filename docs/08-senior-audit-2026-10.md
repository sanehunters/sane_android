## 0. Verdict

The first build (26 chapters, 1,933 items) was **worth keeping and rebuilding around, not discarding**, and
the October pass confirms it:

- Every one of the 917 distinct MASTG/MASWE identifiers cited across the chapters existed in the OWASP
  repositories (verified by filename against local clones of `owasp-mastg` and `maswe`). Nothing was invented.
  What the data audit added is *status*: many of those ids are deprecated v1 pages or placeholders, and the
  chapters are now being moved to the current successors (L14, §B of the chapter handoffs).
- Every item carried the five-field body (`Test / How / Proof / Escalation / Ruled out when`) and the
  five metadata rows. The doctrine (severity, attacker models, breadth-before-depth) is sound and opinionated.
  The target schema is now `docs/11-item-schema.md` (adds `Requires`, `Record`, `Skip when`, the per-chapter
  `## Applicability probe` and `$DEC`), and the chapters were migrated to it mechanically in `203aae1`.

What was broken was the **machinery around the content** and its **fitness for a model operator**, plus a
set of **structural holes** (a missing domain that thirty places pointed at, a phantom domain, an issue tracker
covering 11 of 26 domains, operator-laptop and single-vendor assumptions). The October pass found a second
layer underneath: a staleness check that failed on every day but the generation day, a doctor script that
needed bash 4 on a bash-3.2 host, a fan-out protocol whose ids the writer rejected, gates with vacuous
predicates, a public repository whose doctrine said "push the evidence", and tools whose quoting silently
mangled their probes. Those are fixed or being fixed below.
